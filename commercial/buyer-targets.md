# DealTie buyer target definition

**Current product:** source-linked financial evidence reconciliation across a CIM, Quality of Earnings report and valuation model.  
**Initial segment:** boutique M&A advisory and financial diligence firms.  
**Buyer hypothesis:** managing partner, M&A partner, director or financial diligence lead.  
**Operational user:** senior associate or analyst who prepares or reviews transaction materials.  
**Validation status:** target hypothesis only. No buyer, interview, paid pilot or revenue is claimed.

## Buyer job

Before a transaction pack is circulated or relied upon, check whether selected headline financial metrics remain consistent across the CIM, QoE report and valuation model—and make every unresolved difference traceable to its source.

## Why a buyer may pay

The paid deliverable is not a dashboard. It is a bounded review package:
- confirmed observation ledger with metric, period, value, unit, document and source reference;
- exception register with reported values, spread, tolerance and unit/coverage/reference issues;
- unresolved questions and explicit limitations;
- JSON, CSV and standalone HTML evidence outputs;
- reviewer walkthrough and one correction pass.

The buyer should proceed only if this improves on the firm's existing review process. Value must be established during scoping; do not promise time savings or error prevention in advance.

## Account qualification

Prioritise firms that:
- prepare or review sell-side transaction materials;
- produce CIMs and valuation models or coordinate QoE providers;
- run lean deal teams with repeated manual cross-document checks;
- complete multiple mandates each year;
- can approve a bounded external review through an authorised business representative.

## Buyer roles

| Role | Likely responsibility | Qualification question |
|---|---|---|
| Managing partner / M&A partner | Delivery quality and client risk | Who signs off on consistency across the final pack? |
| Director / VP | Deal execution and review | How are CIM, QoE and model figures tied out before circulation? |
| Senior associate / analyst | Day-to-day tie-out work | Which checks still happen manually, and where are source references retained? |
| Financial diligence lead | QoE and source-data review | How are reported QoE figures reconciled to the CIM and model? |

## Purchase triggers

- A new sell-side mandate reaches CIM/model preparation.
- A revised CIM, QoE report or valuation model must be rechecked.
- A second-pass quality-control review is required before circulation.
- A firm wants a consistent evidence handoff across recurring mandates.

## Disqualifiers

- The firm already handles the workflow adequately.
- The buyer expects automated accounting judgement, semantic matching, formula auditing or a QoE conclusion.
- The buyer requires confidential live deal files to be uploaded to the current prototype.
- No qualified reviewer or authorised contracting route exists.
- The buyer cannot define a bounded set of metrics and periods.

## Commercial model

Start with one paid fixed-scope pilot: up to 15 agreed metrics across up to three source documents, one period/unit convention, source-linked findings, exports, one walkthrough and one correction pass. Fee and delivery schedule are agreed privately after non-confidential scoping.

Repeat work is per subsequent transaction pack or material revision. Do not sell a subscription until a buyer confirms a recurring trigger, owner and budget.

## Research standard

For each target account, record:
1. Official firm website and relevant service line.
2. Public evidence of M&A advisory or financial diligence work.
3. Relevant current role and publicly available business contact route.
4. Why the cross-document workflow may be relevant.
5. Verification date and source URLs.
6. Actual outreach and response status.

A firm name or public contact page is not a qualified lead. A qualified opportunity requires a relevant owner, a recurring workflow pain, a plausible budget owner and agreement to discuss a paid scope.

Any contracting, invoicing, payment, tax, confidentiality and client-data arrangements must be handled by an appropriately authorised adult/business representative. Qualified finance professionals must supervise substantive financial interpretation.
