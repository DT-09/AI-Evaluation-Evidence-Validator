# Buyer discovery and outreach

## Positioning

We provide a narrow, evidence-based verification workstream: reconstruct selected AI performance claims from the evaluation records supplied for review.

We do not replace a technical due-diligence provider or issue an audit/certification opinion.

## Initial outreach

Subject: Specialist verification of AI evaluation claims

Respected [Name],

I reviewed [Firm]'s work in technology due diligence for investors and acquirers.

I am developing a focused assessment that independently recalculates selected AI performance claims from row-level evaluation exports, compares them with reported metrics, and returns a traceable findings register and reproducibility package.

I am trying to understand whether this is already fully covered in your current diligence workflow, or whether independent metric reconstruction remains a manual or difficult-to-reproduce workstream.

Would you be open to a brief discussion about how your team currently verifies AI benchmark claims?

Regards,
[Sender]

## Follow-up after a positive response

Thank you. The intended scope is deliberately narrow: one binary-classification evaluation export, reported metric claims, structural integrity checks, bootstrap uncertainty intervals and a reproducibility package. It does not replace the broader technical review.

If useful, I can share a synthetic example showing the input format, calculation trace and report structure. No client data is needed for that discussion.

## Discovery call structure

1. Ask for their current workflow before showing the product.
2. Ask what inputs are available in a typical deal.
3. Ask which parts are independently recalculated and which are accepted from management/vendor reporting.
4. Ask what makes verification slow, uncertain or difficult.
5. Ask whether the limitation recurs across engagements.
6. Ask what a useful external deliverable would look like.
7. Only then discuss a paid, fixed-scope pilot.

## Qualification

A prospect is qualified only if all are true:

- They handle AI or quantitative systems in an investment, acquisition or vendor diligence context.
- They receive performance claims supported by evaluation exports or can obtain them.
- They identify a recurring verification task that is not already solved adequately.
- A responsible buyer can approve a paid pilot.
- The evidence can be handled lawfully and securely with appropriate adult-authorised contracting and data controls.

## Pipeline stages

1. Researched — fit supported by public information.
2. Contacted — a tailored message sent by an authorised adult/business representative.
3. Replied — substantive response received.
4. Qualified — recurring problem and buyer identified.
5. Scoped — inputs, exclusions, timeline and acceptance criteria agreed.
6. Paid pilot — signed scope and payment arrangement confirmed.
7. Delivered — report and reproducibility package accepted.
8. Repeat — second paid engagement or ongoing channel established.

Do not count an email sent, a connection accepted, a positive comment or a free call as a deal.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim client results, certifications, accuracy guarantees or production readiness.
- Do not request confidential datasets in an initial conversation.
- Do not sign contracts, accept payment, or process confidential client data without appropriate adult involvement and reviewed arrangements.
- Do not provide an investment recommendation.
